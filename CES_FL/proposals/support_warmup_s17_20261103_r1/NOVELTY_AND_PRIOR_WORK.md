# Scoped novelty and prior-work check

Primary pages inspected 3 November 2026, after fixing and launching E/F. This literature note changes no configuration, selection criterion, budget or approval. Abstract-reported performance is an author claim, not our reproduction. This is a focused related-work check, not proof that no equivalent method exists.

| Work and inspected primary evidence | Relevant existing contribution | Implication for CES-FL |
|---|---|---|
| [Group DRO, Sagawa et al., ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08731v2) | Worst-group risk optimization and the importance of regularization for group generalization. | Reweighting or prioritizing difficult groups is established. A future matched standard group-DRO baseline matters for a paper; the present two-candidate cap does not authorize another run. |
| [DAID, Fair Deepfake Detectors Can Generalize, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02645v1) | Demographic rebalancing, subgroup normalization and feature aggregation/alignment address fairness/generalization. | Balanced exposure and demographic-insensitive features alone are not a distinct novelty claim. |
| [Rethinking Individual Fairness in Deepfake Detection, ACM MM 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14326v2), section5 | Anchor/residual inputs, patch shuffling and frequency representation seek manipulation-aware similarity rather than semantic similarity. | “Forensic similarity rather than appearance similarity” is already studied. Our candidate distinction is matched treatment-versus-processing-control score response within the same operator/strength, not the general motivation. This distinction still needs causal validity and competitive evidence. |
| [Ding et al., current paper v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10150v5) | Sensitive-channel decoupling and group distribution alignment. | Aligning demographic groups is not new; our response distribution is a different proposed object from absolute output/features, but additive group score offsets can cancel. See the independent executable fixture and comparison audit. |
| [Face-Fairness / FFT, June2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09881v1) | Post-hoc logit remapping conditioned on frozen face embeddings, plus worst-group variants. | Fairness gains can arise through calibration rather than cue learning. Our study deliberately keeps one global validation-selected threshold; it must not attribute every thresholded gap change to better forensic evidence. |
| [FairReL, BMVC2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28777v1) | Class-conditional residual mean alignment and wavelet-component decorrelation. | Class conditioning and protecting forensic representation components are established ideas. Our proposed response comparison needs a precise distinction and ablations; no new FairReL computation is authorized here. |
| [Intersectional Invariant Learning, 27October2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37881v1), sections3/4 | IRM/REx over challenging demographic partitions with FPR loss and balanced sampling, evaluated on AI-Face. | Demographic invariance, intersectional FPR control and balanced batches are already direct competing directions. AI-Face results and operating points are not comparable to this FF++ validation pilot. |

The FairForensics publisher page was found, but direct full-text access returned403. Do not infer its exact balancing algorithm, preprocessing or hyperparameters from an unavailable paper. No code or weights for any related model were downloaded or executed.

Our inference: the narrow potentially publishable question is whether support-aware procedural response alignment, with separately matched processing controls, contributes beyond applicable error/BCE/control baselines while retaining detection and all supported-axis utility. The one-sided penalty, contributor discount and alignment ramp are practical optimization revisions; none alone establishes an algorithmic novelty claim. The current recipe does not establish that procedural perturbations reproduce authentic manipulation evidence, and six-source Qwen edits cannot supply broad matched forensic coverage.

To support a stronger claim later requires adequate independently reviewed interventions, defensible labels/support, fixed held-out evaluation, uncertainty and matched alternatives (including standard robust training where feasible). This pilot is seed17 only; no training-seed uncertainty or current-method ResNet-50 result is available. Novelty and superiority remain unestablished even if software gates pass or one validation metric improves.
